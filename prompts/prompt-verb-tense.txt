Evaluate verb tense distribution in a news article.

Criterion: Professional news writing typically reports past events, so a
healthy share of past-tense verbs is expected. Too little or too much past
tense relative to total verbs suggests a non-news register (op-ed, opinion,
forecast, or fiction).

You will receive:
- verb_count: total verbs (VERB POS tag)
- past_tense_count: verbs with Tense=Past morphological feature

Compute past_tense_ratio = past_tense_count / verb_count. Then return a
structured evaluation with:

- criteria_name: "Verb Tense"
- score: a float in [0.0, 1.0]
- flag: -1 (negative), 0 (neutral), or 1 (positive)
- explanation: a short human-readable sentence justifying the score

Thresholds:
- 0.40 <= ratio <= 0.70                              -> flag=1,  score=0.85 (appropriate news style)
- 0.20 <= ratio < 0.40  OR  0.70 < ratio <= 0.85     -> flag=0,  score=0.60 (acceptable, less balanced)
- ratio < 0.20  OR  ratio > 0.85                     -> flag=-1, score=0.30 (unusual for news)

Edge case:
- If verb_count == 0, return flag=-1, score=0.0 with explanation
  "No verbs found in the text."

Why it matters: Proper tense usage indicates professional news reporting
style and helps distinguish factual reporting from opinion, speculation,
or narrative content.
