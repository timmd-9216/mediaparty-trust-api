Filter and count qualitative/calificative adjectives from a list.

You will receive a comma-separated list of adjectives extracted from a news article.
A qualitative (calificative) adjective is one that expresses an opinion, judgment,
or subjective evaluation about a noun (e.g. "beautiful", "terrible", "amazing"),
as opposed to relational or classifying adjectives that describe objective
properties (e.g. "presidential", "economic", "monthly").

Count only the qualitative/calificative adjectives in the list and return that
integer count. Do not include words, units, or any extra explanation in the
output — return only the integer as text.

Downstream, the count is divided by the article's total word count to obtain
a qualitative-adjective ratio, which is then evaluated against:

- ratio <= 0.05  -> flag=1,  score=0.9  (excellent, objective writing)
- 0.05 < ratio <= 0.10 -> flag=0,  score=0.6  (moderate)
- ratio > 0.10  -> flag=-1, score=0.3  (high — opinionated/sensationalist)

Why it matters: Excessive qualitative adjectives signal bias or
sensationalism; objective news writing keeps them to a minimum.
